Connecting Zapier to HubSpot takes about fifteen minutes, but the default configuration
most teams ship will quietly create duplicate contacts and lose campaign attribution
within a month. This guide covers the setup that survives contact with real data.

<Callout variant="shortcut" title="The 30-second version">
Use **Find Contact** (with "create if it doesn't exist" enabled) before **Create or
Update Contact**, deduplicate on email only, and always write an explicit
`lifecyclestage` and lead source. Those three decisions prevent the vast majority of
HubSpot sync problems.
</Callout>

## Which connection method should you use?

Zapier is not always the right answer. If your source app already has a native HubSpot
integration, use it — native connectors sync historical data and handle field mapping
without per-task costs.

## Before you start

You need three things: a Zapier account, a HubSpot account with permission to edit
contact settings, and a decision about which field is your deduplication key. That last
one is not optional — skip it and you will be cleaning up duplicates for months.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Check your HubSpot contact tier first">
HubSpot bills on marketing contacts. An unfiltered sync that imports every form fill,
including spam and test submissions, can push you into the next pricing tier. Add the
filter step described below *before* you turn the Zap on, not after.
</Callout>

## What to monitor after launch

Check the Zap History tab daily for the first week. Look for two patterns: repeated
errors on the same record (a mapping problem) and a gradual rise in task consumption
without a matching rise in contacts (a filter problem).

<Callout variant="tip" title="Set up a failure alert">
In Zapier, open Settings > Notifications and enable Zap error emails. Better still, add
a Slack action on the error path so failures land in a channel someone actually reads.
Silent failures are the reason most CRM syncs drift out of trust.
</Callout>
