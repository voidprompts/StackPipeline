n8n is a workflow automation platform from n8n GmbH that can run on the vendor's cloud or on
your own servers. This review documents what the product's published pricing, licence and
documentation actually commit to, and where those commitments stop.

<Callout variant="warn" title="What this review is and is not">
StackPipeline has **not** run a hands-on test of n8n. There is no test account, no testing
period, no logged hours and no workflow benchmark behind this page, so we do not publish any.
Every claim below is sourced to the vendor's own pricing page, licence file and documentation,
or to G2's aggregated review data, each checked on 23 September 2026. Where a figure is not
published, we say so rather than estimate it. Prices and limits change — verify against the
vendor before purchase.
</Callout>

## Billing per execution, not per step

The single most consequential fact about n8n's pricing is the unit. The vendor bills one
*execution* per complete workflow run, "regardless of how many steps are in the workflow or how
much data it processes," and every published plan lists unlimited steps per execution.

That matters because the dominant alternative model bills per step. A ten-step workflow run
1,000 times a month consumes 1,000 n8n executions but 10,000 task-equivalents on a per-step
platform. The gap widens with every node you add, which is why n8n's cost advantage grows
precisely as workflows get more complex.

Users are also unlimited on every Cloud tier, including the $20/mo Starter — seat count is
simply not a pricing lever here.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. The Business tier is **self-hosted only** — there is
no managed checkout at $800/mo, so the plan that unlocks SSO and Git also hands you the
infrastructure. And the vendor does not publish a concurrency figure for Business, describing it
only as "scaling options"; we have left it blank rather than guess.

The jump from Pro to Business is the steepest in the range: $50 to $800 a month. Companies with
fewer than 20 employees can apply for a published Start-up Plan offering 50% off Business.

## The licence is source-available, not open source

n8n is frequently described as open source. Its licence file does not support that description.

The project ships under the **Sustainable Use Licence v1.0**, which grants a royalty-free licence
to use, copy, distribute and modify the software — then limits it. You may use or modify it "only
for your own internal business purposes or for non-commercial or personal use," and you may
distribute it to others "only if you do so free of charge for non-commercial purposes."

<Callout variant="note" title="The .ee carve-out">
Source files with `.ee.` in the filename or `.ee` in the directory name are excluded from the
Sustainable Use Licence altogether. Using them requires a valid n8n Enterprise licence under a
separate agreement. Content on branches other than `master` is not licensed at all.
</Callout>

For most teams automating their own internal operations, these limits never bite. For anyone
building a product on top of n8n, reselling it, or working under procurement rules that require
an OSI-approved licence, they are decisive. "Fair-code" is the accurate label.

## Where the free edition stops

The free self-hosted Community edition is real and unusually complete: no execution cap, no seat
cap, most features included. Registering an instance by email unlocks folders, debug-in-editor
and custom execution data at no additional cost.

What is not free is the managed product. n8n Cloud has no permanent free tier. The trial runs
14 days with Pro-level features but is capped at 1,000 executions, 5 concurrent executions, a
180-second execution timeout and one team project, and excludes admin roles, named versions,
workflow diffs and the public API. No credit card is required for the Cloud trial.

Several capabilities are also self-hosted only regardless of spend — custom nodes and bash
script steps are both marked that way in the vendor's plan comparison. And a documented set of
features requires a paid plan on any deployment: environments, external secrets, log streaming,
projects, SSO, workflow and credential sharing, and Git version control.

## What reviewers report

n8n holds **4.7 out of 5 from 314 reviews on G2**, with 86% of reviewers awarding five stars and
1% awarding one or two. G2's aggregated themes list ease of use, automation capability and
integrations as the most-mentioned strengths; learning curve and missing features lead the
drawbacks.

G2's head-to-head data puts n8n ahead of the category's largest incumbent on meeting requirements
(9.1 versus 9.0) while trailing on usability (8.4 versus 8.6), ease of setup (8.1 versus 8.6) and
support (8.1 versus 8.4). That shape is consistent across sources: more capable, less immediately
approachable.

## How this score was built

The 4.7 is a rubric score, not a measurement. Each criterion in the breakdown above is weighted
per the published [testing methodology](/how-we-test/) — core capability 30%, ease of use 20%,
integrations 20%, value for money 20%, support 10% — and scored from documented capabilities and
third-party aggregate ratings rather than our own hands-on use. The weighted result is 4.69,
rounded to 4.7, which coincides with n8n's current G2 aggregate.

A score built this way can tell you what a product commits to on paper. It cannot tell you how
the product behaves at 3am when a webhook starts returning 500s, and it should not be read as if
it can. Treat it as a structured summary of public evidence, and weight your own trial above it.
