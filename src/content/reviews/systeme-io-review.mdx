Systeme.io is an all-in-one marketing platform from the French company of the same name,
founded by Aurelien Amacker. It bundles sales funnels, email marketing, online courses,
membership sites, a blog builder, e-commerce checkout and affiliate program management into a
single account. This review documents what the product's published pricing page actually
commits to, and where the all-in-one bet gets thin.

<Callout variant="warn" title="What this review is and is not">
StackPipeline has **not** run a hands-on test of systeme.io. There is no test account, no
testing period, no logged hours and no funnel benchmark behind this page, so we do not publish
any. Every claim below is sourced to the vendor's own pricing page or to G2's aggregated review
data, each checked on 28 September 2026. Where a figure is not published, we say so rather than
estimate it. Prices and limits change — verify against the vendor before purchase.
</Callout>

## A free plan that is a product, not a trial

The single most consequential fact about systeme.io's pricing is the free tier. It is
permanent — no credit card, no countdown, no expiry — and it ships a working slice of the full
platform: 2,000 contacts, 3 sales funnels, 1 online course, 1 blog and 1 custom domain, with
**unlimited email sends and unlimited file storage** included. Transaction fees are 0%.

That matters because most competitors in this category gate the entry point behind a paid plan
or a 14-day trial. Systeme.io instead lets you build a funnel, collect a list and sell a first
product at no cost, and only asks for money once the contact count or the funnel and course
caps start to bite.

Two details in that table deserve emphasis. Pricing scales primarily on **contact count**, not
on features — most core tools are available even on the free plan, and the paid tiers mostly
raise the ceilings. And annual billing is presented as roughly two months free: $170, $470 and
$970 for Startup, Webinar and Unlimited respectively.

What the headline pricing table does **not** fully enumerate is the per-tier caps on things like
automation rules and funnel steps. Several of those limits appear only in the detailed plan
comparison rather than the summary, so we have not reproduced numbers we cannot source cleanly
from the vendor — confirm them at signup if heavy automation is central to your use case.

## Where the all-in-one bet gets thin

Consolidation is the whole pitch, and it holds up at the level of core features. It gets thinner
in two places.

<Callout variant="note" title="Integrations run through Zapier">
Beyond payment processors — the pricing page lists Stripe, PayPal, Razorpay, Flutterwave,
Xendit, Apple Pay and MercadoPago — systeme.io has few native integrations. Connecting most
third-party apps means routing through Zapier or webhooks, which adds a paid tool and setup
complexity on top of the platform you chose partly to avoid a sprawling stack.
</Callout>

The second soft spot is polish. Page and email templates are widely described as basic and
dated next to dedicated funnel builders, and the built-in blog lacks the SEO controls a
content-led acquisition strategy needs. Reviewers also note that, as of September 2026, there is
no built-in AI generation for pages, funnels or copy — you build everything manually.

## What reviewers report

Systeme.io holds **4.8 out of 5 from 750 reviews on G2**, with 90% of reviewers awarding five
stars and 7% four stars. G2's aggregated themes put ease of use, affordability and all-in-one
value as the most-mentioned strengths, and responsive support close behind.

The drawbacks in the aggregated data are consistent with the product's shape: reviewers cite
confusing email setup, complex domain configuration and occasional account-verification friction
rather than missing core features. That pattern — beginner-friendly and cheap, but rough at the
edges and shallow in any single specialty — recurs across sources.

## How this score was built

The 4.5 is a rubric score, not a measurement. Each criterion in the breakdown above is weighted
per the published [testing methodology](/how-we-test/) — core capability 30%, ease of use 20%,
integrations 20%, value for money 20%, support 10% — and scored from the vendor's documented
capabilities and third-party aggregate ratings rather than our own hands-on use. Integrations
is deliberately the lowest component because the native connector set is thin.

A score built this way can tell you what a product commits to on paper. It cannot tell you how
the editor feels after a week of building real funnels in it, and it should not be read as if it
can. Treat it as a structured summary of public evidence, and weight your own trial above it.
