No single data provider should be assumed to match every record. Coverage varies with
geography, company size, seniority, source data and the field being requested. A waterfall
can improve coverage by trying multiple providers in sequence, but the outcome depends on
overlap, accuracy and billing rules. Benchmark it on a representative sample before rollout.

## How a waterfall works

A waterfall queries providers in sequence. Provider one is asked for the field; if it
returns no result, provider two is asked, and so on until a result is returned or the list
is exhausted. Billing varies: a provider may charge for attempted lookups, successful
matches, credits consumed, or a combination. Confirm no-match billing before estimating cost.

<Callout variant="tip" title="Optimize for verified value, not raw hit rate">
Start with providers that offer a useful balance of accuracy, incremental matches, cost
and latency on your target segment. A high raw return rate is not a win if the results
are inaccurate or the calls are expensive.
</Callout>

## Ordering the sequence

Hit rate divided by effective cost can be a useful starting heuristic, but it is not a
universal ordering rule. Include match accuracy, overlap between providers, charges for
failed lookups, latency and the value of a correct result. Compare each provider's
incremental verified matches on the same sample before deciding its position.

Segment the waterfall where your data is heterogeneous. If geography, company size or
seniority changes the provider's yield, measure those segments separately and use an
ordering that reflects your own validated results.

## What it costs

Calculate total spend using each provider's actual billing rules, including unsuccessful
attempts and platform credits. Divide by manually verified matches—not merely returned
fields—to estimate cost per correct contact. A waterfall may improve or worsen that cost
compared with a single provider, so compare both approaches on the same list, with the same
field definitions and validation method. Record the sample size, date, segment mix and
verification process so the estimate can be reproduced.

## Where waterfalls do not help

If your list is homogeneous and one provider performs well on a representative, manually
validated sample, a waterfall may add complexity for little gain. Choose a sample large
enough to represent your segments and make the result useful for the decision.

<Callout variant="warn" title="Verify, do not trust reported match rates">
A provider's returned result is not necessarily correct. Manually verify a random sample
of records before relying on a headline match rate, and document the sample size, field
being checked and criteria for a valid match. Reported coverage and verified accuracy are
different measures.
</Callout>
